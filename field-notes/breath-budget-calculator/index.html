How much time does a lyric phrase leave for a breath?

Use a phone recording and this worksheet to estimate room for breathing between two phrases at a fixed tempo. This example assumes four beats per bar and a steady tempo.

Try it aloud

  1. Tap the music at the intended tempo. Read or sing the phrase three times and use the middle duration to avoid tuning for your quickest take.
  2. When the phrase leaves little time, shorten the preceding words or divide at a place the listener can hear as a complete idea.
  3. Repeat with one slower take. Listen for names and final consonants, especially if they land against a drum hit or a chord change.

At 100 BPM two bars last 4.80 seconds. A 4.20-second phrase leaves 0.60 seconds. Use that as a working estimate and check the phrasing in the actual take.
