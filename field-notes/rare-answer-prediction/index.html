Will anyone else pick it?

Use these three practice turns to test how well you predict other people’s answers. Gather a small group. Give everyone the same category and a blank slip. Before revealing the slips, write down how likely you think it is that at least one other person chose your answer.

After the reveal, count a match only for the same answer. Agree on spellings and synonyms before starting. A probability of 75% means you expect a match about three times in four comparable turns.

Choose a forecast and an outcome for at least one turn.

Worked example. Predict a 75% chance of a match and observe a match: the squared error is (0.75 − 1)² = 0.0625. If nobody matches, it is (0.75 − 0)² = 0.5625. Average completed turns. Lower is better. Three turns are enough for a rehearsal; keep more rounds with the same group before drawing a conclusion.
