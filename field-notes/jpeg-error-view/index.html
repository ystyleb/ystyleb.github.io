Compare a JPEG export with its source pixels

A JPEG can look fine at a glance and still blur a thin line or tint a neutral edge. Use the built-in test chart or choose a picture. The two images and the error map use exactly the same grid.

Read the difference

Black on the error map means the decoded pixel matches its reference. A red mark means the red channel changed, in either direction. Mean error summarizes the whole image. Peak error can come from just one pixel. Drag the slider and watch both values alongside the encoded byte count.

The comparison fits the image within 768 × 768 pixels and flattens transparency onto white before encoding. Numbers describe this preview, at the displayed dimensions. For an existing JPEG the reference is its decoded image, so the result measures one additional encoding pass. Processing stays in this browser.
