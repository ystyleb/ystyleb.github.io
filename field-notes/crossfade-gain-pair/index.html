Choose gains at the midpoint of an audio crossfade

A transition can dip when two sounds fade between one another. Move the listening position within a fade and compare linear and equal-power gain pairs. The calculator shows amplitude multipliers that you can reproduce on two editor tracks.

Try the worksheet

Read the result

  1. For two unrelated recordings at comparable levels, an equal-power fade maintains the sum of squared gains. At halfway both channels are about 0.707 of the original amplitude.
  2. Two copies of the same aligned track behave differently. At the midpoint a linear fade adds to 1.000, while an equal-power fade adds to about 1.414 and can clip. Check the mixed peak in your own editor.
  3. Listen once for the trailing consonant and once for the start of the next phrase. Move the edit point if the two sounds conflict, even when the calculated gains look right.
