Budget the bars in a musical dedication

Plan an intro, lyric section and instrumental ending for a song or rap. Count in four beats per bar. Tap a steady pulse while speaking the dedication, then adjust the tempo and number of bars.

Try it on paper

At 96 BPM a bar lasts 2.5 seconds. An intro of two bars gives the listener five seconds before the first word. Eight lyric bars leave twenty seconds for the story. Give each of four lines two bars as a first rehearsal.

Speak a detail in your normal voice over that span. If a name crosses the next downbeat, remove a filler word before raising the tempo. Generated arrangements can use pickups or stretch syllables; check against the actual recording.

Where to try it

  • Songifted — Choose one small scene for a personal song, and rehearse where its important name or place falls.
  • MakeItRap — Try the same words as a short rap. Leave a beat for a breath before the punch line.
