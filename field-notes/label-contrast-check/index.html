Check whether a label reads before you export

Compare two flat colors before committing to a caption, watermark, panel label or small interface badge. Start with a text color and a background color, then preview the combination as a caption and a larger label.

Check the most difficult spot

  1. Pick two colors from a representative export. Choose the least clear area behind the label if the background is a photo.
  2. Try a solid backing when the underlying image changes across crops or panels. Recheck the actual export after compression.
  3. Confirm the label at its smallest intended size. Use position or text alongside color to indicate priority or state.

The calculator compares opaque, flat sRGB colors. Its contrast ratio does not account for transparency, image textures, resizing, font rendering or viewer conditions. See the W3C contrast guidance for interpretation.

Practice examples

I run the tools linked in this section. You can use your own samples for every exercise above.

  • Emote Resizer — Make an emote export and check whether the face still reads in a square crop.
  • TryOnSwap — Choose a garment preview and protect the area that shows the garment’s edge or fasteners.
  • PawMotif — Use a pet portrait and check whether both eyes and the ear tips still fit.
  • Free Image Merger — Make a comparison panel and keep its most important seam or label in view.
